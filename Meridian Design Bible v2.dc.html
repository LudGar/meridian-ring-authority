00 — Executive Design Summary

CategoryDefinition
Name of the ringworldMRA-01 "Meridian"
BuilderMeridian Ring Authority (MRA), an inter-governmental construction and operating authority
Primary functionPermanent habitat for a civilisation; at the same time infrastructure, industry, research and transport network
Construction period[DD] about 240 years from first segment to closed ring, operated for further centuries
Technological level[DD] Advanced but human: no exotic materials shown as fact; robots everywhere, people still on site
Social modelCivil sector councils for daily life; the MRA as a technical authority for everything below the surface
Primary design philosophyThe world is the top layer of a machine. Technology is hidden or integrated on the surface and grows more visible with every level down.
Visual identityCeramic bone surfaces, 45° chamfers, long horizontal bands, colour by function
Main materialsStructural ceramic composite, graphite hull composite, slate-coated steel, civil concrete, glass
Primary coloursCeramic Bone #D9D3C6 · Hull Graphite #2B2F34
Secondary coloursSlate #6B7279 · Signal Orange #E0612A · Conduit Teal #3FD0C0 · Service Blue #3F6F9A
Recognisable design featureThe 45° chamfered corner plus a teal energy line at belt height, on every building, vehicle and street lamp

Design thesis

Meridian looks like a natural world from above and like a machine from the side. Every visible object belongs to one family: chamfered, banded, bone-coloured, coded by function. Technology is hidden at the surface and grows more visible with every level down. Panels, buildings and vehicles show their age, because the ring has been built and repaired over centuries.

ParameterDesign decisionReasoning

Ring width[DD] 500 km habitableAllows several biome belts side by side across the width.

Panel design grammar

  • Basic shape: rectangle with a 45° chamfer at the corners on panels under 4 m. Large panels stay rectangular.
  • Module size: 1 : 2 : 4 : 20 m. Street furniture uses 1 m, the rim uses 4 m rows, caverns use a 20 m grid.
  • Joints: 4 cm shadow gap with a 1 cm light reveal on the upper edge.
  • Fixing: four round fasteners, inset by 1/14 of the panel width.
  • Lighting: teal only for energy, warm white only where people work.
  • Markings: orange only where hands or tools may touch: access panels, handles, hatch rims.
  • Ageing: bone ceramic yellows to the aged tone #B3A68C, with hairline cracks and stains.
  • Repair marks: replacement panels from a newer generation sit among aged ones; a decommissioned panel is left in hull graphite.

Lighting language

  • Public: warm white (#F6E3B8), downward only, from luminaires at the end of an arm.
  • Technical: teal (#3FD0C0) lines, only where energy flows.
  • Security: teal rings on sensors, a 360° glow at 1.2 m height.
  • Maintenance: warm floodlights plus orange beacons on moving equipment.
  • Emergency: orange pulsing strips at floor level, leading to the nearest P-05.
  • Architectural: soffit lines under canopies, never uplighting on facades.

Shared rules for M-01 to M-08.

  • Exterior: an aged-ceramic plinth, a bone body and an orange crown or band.

Logo concepts

A — Meridian (favourite)
Shape: a ring crossed by a vertical bar. Symbolism: the ring, and the meridian line where the surface meets the shaft. Geometry: stroke 1/7 of the diameter, bar 1/7. Small scale: works at 12 px. Industrial: stencil cut, bar in orange. Digital: the bar pulses as a loader.
B — Segment
Shape: an octagonal ring with one segment in orange. Symbolism: segments built one by one. Geometry: 45° throughout. Small scale: the orange segment gets lost below 16 px. Industrial: 8 plates. Digital: segments light up in sequence.
C — Strata
Shape: three horizontal bars in a disc. Symbolism: surface, utility and structure layers. Geometry: bars 1/10 of the diameter. Small scale: bars merge below 20 px. Industrial: three painted bands. Digital: bars as level indicators.

Favourite: A, Meridian. It is the simplest of the three, still reads at stencil and 12 px sizes, and already appears on every model (vehicle doors, the security robot's chest). The bar repeats the vertical access shafts, which are what the organisation is really responsible for.

13 — Wayfinding System

  • Colours: the header band says who a sign is for (graphite = public, orange = maintenance, teal = energy, blue = safety).
  • Symbols: drawn on a 45° grid with a stroke of 1/8 of the symbol height.
  • Arrows: chamfered shaft, 90° head; down arrows mean "level below".
  • Level marking: L0–L6 in a graphite octagon at every stair, lift and hatch.
  • Sector numbers: two digits, always in the top-left corner.
  • Building ID: on a graphite plate at door height, right of the entrance.
  • Emergency exits: orange pulsing floor strips that lead to the nearest P-05.
  • Maintenance access: an orange ring on the floor and on the hatch.

19 — Master Style Prompt

Grounded hard-surface concept art of a human-built ringworld operated by the Meridian Ring Authority. Architecture: chamfered 45° corners, octagonal and banded building masses, long horizontal window bands on civil buildings, narrow vertical slits on technical buildings, cylindrical plant drums on roofs, towers tapering in segments. Materials: matte ceramic in bone (#D9D3C6) with visible ageing to a yellowed tone, graphite composite hulls (#2B2F34), slate powder-coated steel (#6B7279), board-formed concrete, clear glass, black polymer seals. Colour language: bone means public, solid signal orange (#E0612A) means maintenance and hands-on access, orange-graphite diagonal stripes mean danger, teal (#3FD0C0) glows only where energy flows, service blue (#3F6F9A) marks water and rescue. Lighting: warm white downlight for people, teal lines for energy, orange beacons on moving equipment, no random neon. Signage: condensed capital sans with technical mono codes, bone plates with a coloured header band, sector, level and ID numbers everywhere. Panel design: rectangular modules on 1, 4 and 20 m grids, shadow-gap joints, four corner fasteners, mixed generations and repaired patches. Infrastructure: perfectly straight roads and tracks, service lanes edged in orange, hatches with orange rings, kiosks that hide deep shafts, utilities layered below ground. Scale: always include 1.8 m people, vehicles and bridges, and let the ring's upward-curving horizon show the world is orders of magnitude larger than any building. Human presence: commuters, crews in orange, families in parks. Technological aesthetic: functional, maintained, slightly worn, never glossy. Environmental integration: natural landscapes on top, geometry visible at the edges, the machine growing more visible with every level down.

20 — Design Rules

  1. Technology is functional, not ornamental.
  2. Every large technical structure must have a recognisable purpose.
  3. Infrastructure belongs to the architecture.
  4. Maintenance access is part of the world, never hidden behind fake walls.
  5. Nature and technology must touch: a track through a forest, a pump under a lake.
  6. No generic "futuristic" architecture; every form follows the chamfer-and-band rule.
  7. No direct Halo copy.
  8. Corners are chamfered at 45°.
  9. Colour carries meaning; never add a colour for decoration.
  10. Teal glows only where energy flows.
  11. Orange marks anything a hand or tool may touch.
  12. Every object carries an ID code.
  13. Every image shows age: new, used, repaired and decommissioned side by side.
  14. Every image includes a human for scale.
  15. Technology becomes more visible the deeper you go.

21 — Anti-Design / What to Avoid

  • Direct Halo copies
  • Known Halo symbols and glyph patterns
  • Covenant architecture: organic curves, purple, bioluminescence
  • UNSC logos or military olive drab
  • Generic cyberpunk: rain-soaked neon, holograms
  • Over-clean, spotless sci-fi cities
  • Purely decorative technology
  • Random neon lighting
  • Interchangeable futuristic buildings
  • Illogical infrastructure: pipes that go nowhere
  • Technical elements without a function
  • Large radii and blob shapes
  • Glowing lines in any colour other than teal
  • Panels that are all identical and all new
  • Scenes without a scale reference
  • Sky-high gleaming chrome

22 — Final Design DNA

01 — The world is the top layer. Everything residents see sits on a machine two kilometres deep.
02 — Chamfer, never round. The 45° corner is the signature, from lamp post to spire.
03 — Bands carry the eye. Long horizontals on buildings, vehicles and rims.
04 — Colour is a language. Bone public, orange hands-on, teal energy, blue water.
05 — Every object has an ID. If it can break, it has a code.
06 — Age is visible. Five eras stand side by side.
07 — Access is everywhere. Hatches, kiosks and spires are part of daily life.
08 — Scale through people. A 1.8 m figure in every frame.
09 — Depth reveals the machine. The deeper the level, the more honest the technology.
10 — Built by people, kept by people. A world that works because someone maintains it.
